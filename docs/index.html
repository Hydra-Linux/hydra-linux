The Goal

Hydra Linux aims to be a universal distribution for everyone — from developers and power users to people just getting started with Linux. It proves that you don't need to choose between control and convenience, and that OpenRC doesn't mean worse or less user-friendly. Simple, fast, transparent — that's the goal.

What is Hydra Linux?

A rolling, source-based distribution built for everyone — not just experts. It gives you control, performance, and transparency without getting in your way.

🔄 Rolling, never reinstalling

Install once, update forever. No version upgrades — just a continuous stream of the latest software.

📦 Source-first, but smart about it

Packages are compiled from source by default, optimized for your hardware. Big packages automatically switch to binaries or pre-compiled parts so you're not waiting hours.

⚡ flash package manager

Every package tool is built in C — fast, minimal dependencies, and designed to be as simple as Arch's pacman but for a source-based workflow.

🎨 Your desktop, your choice

KDE Plasma 6 out of the box, but GNOME, Xfce, and Sway are one checkbox away during installation.

🚀 OpenRC is not a downgrade

Fast, clean, and easy to configure — OpenRC with supervise-daemon gives you full service supervision without the complexity of systemd. User-friendly doesn't require bloat.

🧱 Built from scratch, transparently

Full 4-stage bootstrap from cross-toolchain to running system. Every recipe, every config, every script is open in one repository.

How flash Handles Packages

When you run flash install <package>, it checks the source size and chooses the fastest strategy automatically.

≤ 100 MB

Compiled from Source

Packages under 100 MB are built entirely from source inside a bubblewrap sandbox, optimized for your CPU with the flags you configure.

100–450 MB

Downloaded as Binary

Medium packages use pre-built binaries from the repository. No compilation — just download, verify, and install. Saves hours on large toolchains and browsers.

> 450 MB

Assembled from Parts

Massive packages are split into logical modules during the distro build. Pre-compiled parts are downloaded and assembled locally — faster than a full build, more flexible than a single blob.
